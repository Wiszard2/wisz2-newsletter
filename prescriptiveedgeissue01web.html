The 2027 Budget Season Starts Now: Why Loss Prevention Can't Wait for January

Why the next 90 days decide your LP budget for all of 2027 — plus the data every AP leader needs walking into the room.

Published Tuesday, September 8, 2026 · 5–7 min read
TLDR
  • Retail tech budgets hit $113B in 2026 (Forrester), up 6.6% YoY — but LP teams that show up to budget season without a growth story get cut first, not last.
  • Shoplifting incidents are down 12.4% year over year (NRF). That sounds like good news. It's actually the hardest sentence in retail right now — because it's the one a CFO uses to justify a flat LP budget.
  • Self-checkout shrink runs 16x higher than staffed lanes. AI exception detection cuts shrink 20–30% where retailers have actually deployed it. That gap between those two numbers is the entire budget conversation this fall.
  • The leaders who win Q4 budget season aren't pitching "we stopped $X in loss." They're pitching "we protected margin and funded next year's growth." Different pitch. Different outcome.

The trap every LP leader is about to walk into

Here's the sentence that's going to cost some of you your 2027 budget: "Shoplifting is down."

It's true — incidents dropped 12.4% year over year, per NRF's 2026 research. It is also, if you're not careful, the exact data point your CFO uses in October to ask why Loss Prevention needs the same headcount and the same tech spend it had last year.

That's the trap. Declining theft numbers read as "problem solved" to anyone who isn't in the store every day. They don't read as "the threat moved" — which is what's actually happening. Organized retail crime doesn't respect the store walls that used to contain it. Fraud is migrating into self-checkout, into returns, into channels the old shrink numbers were never built to see. The people writing the budget checks are looking at a headline. You're looking at a moving target. Those are two different conversations, and right now, most LP leaders are still having the wrong one.

What the data actually says

A few numbers worth having memorized before your next budget meeting:

  • $113Btotal 2026 U.S. retail tech budget, up 6.6% YoY. Software now takes 46 cents of every dollar spent.Forrester, 2026
  • 12.4%drop in shoplifting incidents, 2024–2025. Fraud is shifting channels, not disappearing.NRF, 2026
  • 16xthe shrink rate at self-checkout versus a staffed register.Industry data, 2026
  • 20–30%typical shrink reduction from a well-implemented AI exception-detection system.Industry data, 2026

Put those last two together and you get the actual pitch: self-checkout is where the money is leaking, and AI-driven exception detection is the fastest-proven way to plug it. That's not a "stop theft" argument. That's a margin-recovery argument — and margin-recovery arguments get funded even in flat-budget years.

The reframe that wins the room

Every LP budget request in the last decade has been built the same way: here's what we prevented, please don't cut us. That framing put LP in a permanent defensive crouch, competing for scraps against departments that get to talk about growth.

The leaders who win this budget season are doing something different. They're walking in with a number, not an apology — "here's the shrink we're leaving on the table at self-checkout, here's what closing that gap is worth, here's the technology that gets us there." That's a growth pitch wearing a loss-prevention badge, and it's the difference between LP getting funded in Q4 and LP getting flat-lined into Q1.

We'll spend the next two issues going deep on exactly how to build that number and that pitch — starting next week with what the 2026 NRF data actually tells you once you stop reading the headline and start reading the appendix.

If you're a solution provider reading this

Your buyers are having this exact budget conversation right now, whether or not you're in the room for it. The vendors who show up in September with a margin-recovery case — tied to a specific, quantified gap like self-checkout shrink — are the ones getting a line item in the 2027 budget. The vendors who show up in Q1 with a generic "reduce shrink" pitch are competing for whatever's left. Timing your outreach to this cycle isn't optional anymore. It's the whole game.

The move this week

Three things worth doing before your next budget conversation, not after:

  • Pull your self-checkout shrink number specifically — not blended shrink, not total shrink. The 16x gap is where the budget case lives.
  • Price the gap, not the problem. What is closing even a third of that gap worth in annual margin? That's your headline number, not "incidents prevented."
  • Write the pitch as a growth memo, not a defense memo. If your budget ask reads like an apology, rewrite it before it reaches the CFO's desk.
